The big picture

After place & route a digital tile is streamed out as layout geometry. Four different checks look at it. DRC and EDRC only look at the drawn shapes. LVS and ERC first extract a circuit (devices and nets) from the shapes and then judge that circuit. Click a box to jump to that check.

Rule of thumb: DRC/EDRC = "can it be made?"   LVS = "is it the circuit we designed?"   ERC = "is the circuit electrically legal?"

DRC Design Rule Check

Checks the drawn polygons against the foundry's manufacturing rules, one layer or layer pair at a time:

  • Width / spacing — wires too thin or too close (the M17 spacing to the DFTIO macro was this kind)
  • Enclosure / overlap — metal must surround a via by a minimum amount (via enclosure)
  • Area, density, end-of-line
  • Antenna (ANT) — a long, floating metal run can charge up during manufacturing and hurt a transistor gate; fixed with diodes or layer hops

Fixes: reroute / widen / move a shape, change a via definition, add fill or diodes. Geometry only: DRC does not know what the wires mean.

EDRC Extra / enhanced design rules

A second geometry check, run on top of the base DRC deck, with additional rules. In your reports these show up as rule names like EDRC_31_M0_SIGNOFF, EDRC_22a_SIGNOFF and EDRC_33_M17_SIGNOFF, in a separate CbTileEDRC report.

  • Same kind of data as DRC: shapes on layers, no netlist.
  • A layout can be DRC-clean and still have EDRC findings.
  • Often these are layer-specific or recommended-style rules; some are waived or only matter at certain levels.
I am inferring the exact definition from the rule names and report layout. Confirm what EDRC_* means in your methodology (read the deck comments or ask the PD lead) before quoting it.

LVS Layout vs Schematic

Extracts a netlist from the layout and compares it to the source netlist (the design's Verilog/spice). They must be the same circuit: same devices, same connections.

  • Short — two nets are touching in layout but are separate in the source: the layout has fewer nets.
  • Open — one net is broken into pieces: the layout has more nets.
  • Missing/extra devices, name or pin mismatches, power/ground connection errors.

Net counts are the quickest tell. In your run17 the top level showed layout 58480 vs source 58481 = one short; run12 was 58477 vs 58477 = clean.

ERC Electrical Rule Check

Looks at the extracted connectivity and flags things that are legal geometry and may even match the netlist, but are electrically unsound:

  • Floating wells / nets — an N-well or P-well with no tap tied to a supply (in your erc.sum: floating.nxwell_float)
  • Missing taps/ties — transistor bulk not connected to VDD/VSS
  • Unconnected inputs, supply shorts, wrong supply on a well

Fix: add the tap / tie cell, or connect the net. It runs from the same extraction as LVS, which is why erc.sum sits in the CbTileLVS folder.

Suggested order

  1. LVS first. A short or open invalidates everything else, and an LVS-clean netlist is the baseline.
  2. DRC next — largest counts first, starting with your own cells and layers.
  3. ERC alongside LVS: it uses the same extraction.
  4. EDRC last, after base DRC is clean; many items are sign-off refinements, some waivable.
